My research sits at the intersection of machine learning and biology. I build the models and the infrastructure behind them: protein language models for T-cell antigen specificity, data pipelines that turn billions of immune receptor sequences into training-ready datasets, and evaluation frameworks that test whether a model generalizes beyond its training distribution. My current focus is agentic AI for drug discovery: systems of LLM agents that plan and carry out multi-omic analyses, with an LLM-as-a-judge layer that evaluates their output before a scientist sees it. My work spans immunology, virology, oncology, and infectious disease.

I am currently the Director of AI/ML at Athos Therapeutics, a clinical-stage biotech company, where I lead the engineering team building that platform.

Before Athos, I spent more than six years at the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center. As a Staff Scientist in the Warren Lab, I led [QUEST](https://github.com/shashidhar22/quest), a project that uses protein language models to predict which targets T-cells recognize. I built the data and training pipeline for 2.6 billion T-cell and target sequences and trained the models on multi-GPU cloud servers. This work was funded by a Yosemite-American Cancer Society Award, where I was Co-PI. I also combined single-cell, bulk RNA-seq, and immune repertoire data to study the immune response to Kaposi sarcoma, and mapped genetic variation across about 1,500 Epstein-Barr virus genomes. Earlier at Fred Hutch, I built LymphoSeq2, an open-source R package for immune repertoire analysis.

I earned my Ph.D. in Bioinformatics from the Georgia Institute of Technology, where I worked with Dr. Fredrik Vannberg and Dr. Eldin Talundzic. During my Ph.D., I worked with the Malaria Branch at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. There I built NeST, a toolkit for tracking malaria drug resistance that became a standard protocol across six CDC laboratories. I also helped assemble a complete genome of the malaria parasite *Plasmodium brasilianum*.
