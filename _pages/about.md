I am the Director of AI/ML at Athos Therapeutics, a clinical-stage biotech company. I set the company's AI strategy and lead the AI/ML work of a small engineering team. We are building an agentic AI platform that helps drug discovery scientists analyze multi-omic data. I work closely with the scientists who use the platform, and I present our work to pharmaceutical partners.

My goal is to build AI tools that help scientists use complex biological data to make better decisions. I care most about tools that people actually adopt in their daily work.

Before Athos, I spent more than six years at the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center. As a Staff Scientist in the Warren Lab, I built protein language models to predict which targets T-cells recognize. This work was funded by a Yosemite-American Cancer Society Award, where I was Co-PI. I also studied the immune response to Kaposi sarcoma and other virus-linked cancers by combining single-cell, bulk RNA-seq, and immune repertoire data. Earlier at Fred Hutch, I built LymphoSeq2, an open-source R package for immune repertoire analysis.

I earned my Ph.D. in Bioinformatics from the Georgia Institute of Technology, where I worked with Dr. Fredrik Vannberg and Dr. Eldin Talundzic. During my Ph.D., I worked with the Malaria Branch at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. There I built NeST, a toolkit for tracking malaria drug resistance that became a standard protocol across six CDC laboratories. I also helped assemble a complete genome of the malaria parasite *Plasmodium brasilianum*.
